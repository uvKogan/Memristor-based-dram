The Deck's Shape

One tangible win today: you can recite the deck's 7-section flow and timing budget from memory, and you can deliver the two newest slides (9 & 10) without reading the speaker notes verbatim.

This lesson assumes the content (numbers, mechanics, defenses) is already solid via the grilling rounds - see Meeting_Prep_Cheat_Sheet.md. Here we're learning the deck: what order things come in, how long each part should take, and how to talk over the two slides that didn't exist until this session.

1. The shape

Seven sections, ~60 minutes, 37 slides total (counting section dividers and the two brand-new ones). Primary source: presentation_deck.html / Presentation_Outline.md.

Two things worth noticing about this shape before you memorize it:

Results is the whale. 15 of 37 slides, 28 of 60 minutes - almost half the talk. If you're running long anywhere, Results is where the time actually went, and it's the one section where cutting is genuinely costly (each subsection - latency, power, scaling, density/endurance, the flagship-claim reframe - earns its own verdict).

Methodology grew by one slide this session. It was 6 slides (~10 min); it's now 7 (~11 min) because Slide 10 ("One Chip → a Full DIMM") is new. That's the section most likely to feel unrehearsed - see §2 below.

Q1. Without scrolling up: how many slides are in Results, and roughly how many minutes?
15 slides, ~28 minutes - the largest section by far.
Q2. Which section grew by one slide this session, and why?
Methodology, 6→7 slides (~10→~11 min) - the new "One Chip → a Full DIMM" numbers slide (Slide 10) was inserted after the pipeline diagram.
Q3. If you're 20 minutes in, which section should you be entering or just finishing?
0.5 + 6 + 6 + 11 = 23.5 min to clear Methodology. At the 20-minute mark you should be mid-Methodology - roughly around the toolchain-audit or validation-scope slides, not yet into Results.

2. The two new slides, cold

Slides 9 and 10 didn't exist before this session. They're the least rehearsed part of the deck - the place you're most likely to reach for notes. Read the actual speaker note once, then try saying it in your own words before you check yourself against it.

Slide 9 - The Pipeline (script-level diagram: 1_run_nvsim_hardware.py → 2_extract_hardware_metrics.py → 3_gen_nvmain_config.py → 4_execute_simulation.py, orchestrated by mbmm_master.py)

Say (verbatim from the deck) "NVSim only ever characterizes ONE chip - that's what crosses the boundary at step 1→2. Everything from step 3 onward is NVMain replicating that single chip a small, fixed number of times and wrapping it in its own controller; NVSim never sees a rank, a controller, or a workload. This bridge is itself a contribution - most tools stop at one layer or the other."
Q4. In one sentence, in your own words: why do you need two simulators here, not one?
NVSim knows device physics but nothing about systems (no controller, no workload); NVMain knows controllers and workloads but nothing about circuit physics - neither one alone can answer "how does this chip behave as a DIMM under a real trace."

Slide 10 - One Chip → a Full DIMM (stat callouts: 1 Gb chip · 1–64 chip replication · 800 MHz ReRAM clock · 64-bit bus · 8/16 GB full-DIMM capacity · 3 GHz matched-host CPUFreq)

Say (verbatim from the deck) "These are the numbers to have cold if asked 'how big is this system, really.' NVSim gives you one 1 Gb chip; NVMain's own controller and address-mapping logic - not NVSim - is what turns up to 64 of them into an 8–16 GB DIMM."
Q5. Someone asks "so is this a real DIMM you built, or a simulation of one?" - what's your one-line answer, using this slide?
It's a simulated DIMM: one real, literature-characterized chip (NVSim) replicated up to 64x and driven by NVMain's own controller/address-mapping logic - the replication and system behavior are simulated, not fabricated.

3. Where this leaves you

You now have the map (§1) and the two weakest slides rehearsed (§2). What this lesson deliberately does not cover: word-for-word delivery of every slide in Results (too much for one sitting - that content is already solid via the cheat sheet, and Results' speaker notes are the same style as Slides 9/10 above, so the same "read once, say it back" drill applies if you want to run it yourself section by section).
